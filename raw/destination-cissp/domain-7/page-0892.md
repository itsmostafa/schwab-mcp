Cyclic Redundancy Check (CRC)
Understand the purpose behind the use of checksums/CRC
In the context of backups and moving data around a network, a term known as cyclic redundancy
check, or CRC, is often used. A CRC is a bit of math that is used to ensure the integrity of data, and
CRC can be used anywhere data resides—hard drive, when moving data across a network, and even in
RAM. Among other uses, a CRC can be used to validate backups in order to ensure the integrity of the
backed up data. A CRC is a method of detecting accidental changes to data at rest or in motion.
7.10.3 Spare Parts
CORE CONCEPTS
 Spare parts strategies include: cold spare, warm spare, hot spare.
 Which strategy is used is often dependent upon organizational goals
and objectives.
 Key considerations include: cost, criticality.
Oftentimes people take for granted that systems in a data center “just work” all the time. But what
happens when those systems stop working due to a part failing? Business stops. So, with any critical
systems, it’s important to have spares of critical components for those systems on hand. Critical
components include things like power supplies, cooling fans, hard drives, and so on. Then, if the
primary part in a system fails, a spare part can be installed and up and running very quickly. Three
types of spare parts exist, and the name points to the location. Table 7-13 contains a summary of all
spare types.
