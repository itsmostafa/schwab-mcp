Discretionary Access Control (DAC), as the word discretionary implies,
means somebody determines who can access an asset. That somebody is the
owner. The defining characteristic of DAC is that access is given, based upon
the owner’s discretion as also shown in Figure 5-13. That’s considered a great
security best practice, since owners are accountable for and are therefore in
the best position to determine who should access those assets.

Within the realm of discretionary access control, three primary types of DAC
exist: 1. Rule-Based Access Control: Access to an object by a given subject
is based upon one or more rules, determined by the owner.
