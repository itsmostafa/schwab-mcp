Least privilege: A classic example of overprivileged
accounts exists in a variety of companies today where
several people (if not the whole company) just have local
administrator permissions on their machines. This goes
against least privilege. Most people in a company don’t
need to have local administrator permissions, and hence to
apply least privilege, a group policy should state that
everyone has standard accounts configured, apart from a
handful of administrators. Least privilege means you are
given only the absolute level of access that you need, and
nothing more. If you only need read access, that’s all you are
given.
