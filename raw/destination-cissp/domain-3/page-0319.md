The term Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the multitude of devices that are connected to the internet
and probably shouldn’t be connected to it. IoT is the concept that tiny processing computers, the
network cards that allow you to connect a device to the internet, have become so cheap that
manufacturers are putting them in everything, including refrigerators, washing machines, dryers, cars,
toasters, and so on. It seems that some type of network functionality is being installed in most similar
devices these days. What’s the risk here? Manufacturers are installing computer and networking
technology into the things they build, but the technology being installed is mass-produced; the
computer and networking technology is made very cheaply and contains little to no security. After a
refrigerator is purchased, how often is built-in technology upgraded? How often is the firmware
upgraded on a fridge? The answer to both questions is likely “Never.” Nobody thinks about patching
