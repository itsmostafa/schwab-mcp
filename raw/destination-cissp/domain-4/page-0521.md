Transmission of Digital Data over Analog Connections
When telephone communication was first being envisioned,
scientists and engineers determined that because the human
voice is analog, analog frequencies should be used for
telephone communication. However, as technology advanced
and data demands grew, the need for better communication
technology also increased. Data doesn’t travel well over analog
frequencies, though a temporary fix came in the form of
modems. A modem, which stands for modulator/demodulator,
takes data and converts it to analog, so that it can be
transmitted across the analog telephone network. Once it
arrives at its destination, the analog signal is converted back to
data by the receiving modem as depicted in Figure 4-6. As was
