# COBIT — Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies

COBIT is a framework developed by **ISACA** for IT governance and management. It provides
principles and tools to help organizations govern IT effectively in alignment with business goals.

> **Source gap**: The *destination-cissp* source defers Security Control Frameworks (including
> COBIT) to Domain 3 (§3.3.1). This page is a stub to be enriched when Domain 3 is ingested.
> The source does reference COBIT briefly alongside NIST, ISO, SABSA, and PCI as control frameworks.

## Key Facts

- Current version: **COBIT 2019**.
- Developed and maintained by **ISACA**.
- Focus: IT **governance** and **management** aligned to business objectives.
- Bridges the gap between business requirements, IT risks, and IT controls.

## COBIT 2019 Principles (6 principles)

1. Provide stakeholder value
2. Holistic approach
3. Dynamic governance system
4. Governance distinct from management
5. Tailored to enterprise needs
6. End-to-end governance system

## Governance vs. Management in COBIT

- **Governance** (Board/executives): Evaluates, Directs, Monitors (EDM domain).
- **Management** (executives/IT): Plans, Builds, Runs, Monitors (PBRM domains: APO, BAI, DSS, MEA).

## ISACA Risk IT Framework

Companion to COBIT specifically for risk optimization. Latest version:
- Aligns with COBIT 2019.
- Greater emphasis on cybersecurity.
- Contains guidelines for risk optimization, security, and business value.

## Exam-Relevant Nuance

- COBIT is primarily an **IT governance** framework (vs. NIST RMF which is a risk management framework).
- On the exam, COBIT is associated with **alignment of IT/security with business objectives** — the governance alignment concept.
- When a question asks which framework focuses on **IT governance aligned to business goals** → likely COBIT.
