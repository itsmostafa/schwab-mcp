# Professional Ethics and ISC2 Code of Ethics

Ethics form the foundation of the security profession. The ISC2 Code of Ethics governs all CISSP
holders globally, and agreement with it is a **condition of certification**.

## Key Facts

### ISC2 Code of Ethics Preamble (paraphrased)
> "The safety and welfare of society and the common good, duty to our principals, and to each
> other, requires that we adhere, and be seen to adhere, to the highest ethical standards of
> behavior. Therefore, strict adherence to this Code is a condition of certification."

### The Four Canons — in order (memorize this order)

| # | Canon |
|---|-------|
| 1 | Protect society, the common good, necessary public trust and confidence, and the infrastructure. |
| 2 | Act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly, and legally. |
| 3 | Provide diligent and competent service to principals. |
| 4 | Advance and protect the profession. |

### Organizational Code of Ethics
- Consistent ethical behavior in an organization requires codification in **policies** (corporate laws).
- Ethical behavior must be driven by management and instilled through top-down enforcement.

## Exam-Relevant Nuance

- **Order of the canons matters for conflict resolution.** If a scenario presents a conflict between canons, apply them in numbered order — society/common good (Canon 1) always takes precedence over advancing the profession (Canon 4).
- Canon 1 places *society* above your own organization or clients ("principals" in Canon 3). On the exam, if an action protects your employer but harms the public, Canon 1 overrides.
- Canon 3 mentions "principals" — this means employers, clients, and those who engage you. It is **not** the top priority.
- "Seen to adhere" in the Preamble: perception matters. Even the appearance of unethical behavior is prohibited.

## Mnemonic

**"Protect, Act, Provide, Advance"** (order: society → honorable → principals → profession)
