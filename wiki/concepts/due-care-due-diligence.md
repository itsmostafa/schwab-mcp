# Due Care and Due Diligence

Due care and due diligence are complementary concepts that appear frequently on the exam and
are a common source of confusion. They are **not** synonymous.

## Definitions (Table 1-6)

| Due Care | Due Diligence |
|---|---|
| **Accountable protection of assets** based on and aligned with the goals and objectives of the organization. | **The ability to prove due care** to stakeholders — upper management, regulators, customers, shareholders. |
| "Doing the right thing" | "Proving you did the right thing" |
| Implementing controls | Demonstrating controls are working |

### Classic Example: Penetration Testing

- **Due care** = owner requests and authorizes a penetration test, then authorizes remediation of identified vulnerabilities.
- **Due diligence** = providing proof (reports, metrics) to management and customers that vulnerabilities were remediated in a cost-effective way.

## Key Facts

- Due care is the **action** (implementing security controls appropriate to the risk).
- Due diligence is the **evidence/proof** that due care is being exercised on an ongoing basis.
- Failure to exercise due care can result in **negligence** — legal liability for security breaches.
- Due diligence involves activities like audits, assessments, penetration tests, monitoring, and reporting — all aimed at proving that due care exists.
- Risk ignorance (choosing to ignore an identified risk) violates both due care and due diligence.

## Exam-Relevant Nuance

- Exam trap: "An organization implements a firewall" — this is **due care**. "An organization has its firewall audited annually and provides audit reports to regulators" — this is **due diligence**.
- A company that suffers a breach may be liable if they cannot demonstrate either (a) they took appropriate protective measures (due care) or (b) they regularly verified those measures worked (due diligence).
- Due diligence is what regulators, auditors, and customers require to gain confidence in an organization's security posture.
