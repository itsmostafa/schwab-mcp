# Intellectual Property

Intellectual property (IP) is any intangible product of human intellect that law protects from
unauthorized use. IP laws encourage creation of intellectual goods while protecting creators.

## Types of Intellectual Property (Table 1-7)

| Type | Protects | Disclosure Required? | Duration | Protects Against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Trade Secret** | Business information (formulas, processes, customer lists) | No | Potentially infinite | Misappropriation |
| **Patent** | Functional innovations; novel inventions | Yes | Set period of time | Making, using, or selling the invention |
| **Copyright** | Expression of an idea in a fixed medium (books, movies, songs, code) | Yes | Set period of time | Copying or substantially similar work |
| **Trademark** | Color, sound, symbol, or name distinguishing a product/company | Yes | Potentially infinite | Creating consumer confusion |

## Key Facts

- **Trade secrets** require NO disclosure — secrecy itself is the protection mechanism.
- **Patents** require public disclosure in exchange for a time-limited monopoly on the invention.
- **Copyrights** protect the expression of an idea, not the idea itself. (You can't copyright the concept of a detective novel, only your specific text.)
- **Trademarks** protect brand identity elements (logos, slogans, etc.).
- The goal of all IP law is to **encourage creation** by protecting creators' economic interests.

## Import/Export Controls (§1.4.3)

- **Wassenaar Arrangement**: International treaty managing export of dual-use goods (commercial and military potential), including cryptography. Member states can exchange strong cryptography; non-members cannot.
- **ITAR (International Traffic in Arms Regulations)**: US law controlling export of items on the US Munitions List (USML). Administered by the State Department (DDTC).
- **EAR (Export Administration Regulations)**: US law controlling commercial-use items (computers, lasers, etc.) with potential military applications. Administered by the Commerce Department (Bureau of Industry and Security).

## Exam-Relevant Nuance

- Trade secret is the only form of IP that requires **no disclosure**; it relies entirely on keeping the information secret.
- Trade secrets and trademarks can be **indefinitely protected** (as long as maintained); patents and copyrights expire.
- On the exam, software is protected by **copyright** (the code as expression), while software patents protect the underlying method or algorithm (if eligible).
