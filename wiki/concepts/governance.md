# Security Governance Principles

Governance is the act of directing an organization to achieve its goals and objectives, with the
ultimate purpose of **increasing organizational value**. Security governance must be aligned with
and subordinate to corporate governance.

## Key Facts

- **Corporate governance** — Activities and initiatives the organization undertakes to increase value (new processes, products, services, risk reduction, compliance, etc.).
- **Security governance** — All activities/programs the security function drives or supports, always aligned with corporate governance goals.
- **Top-down, not bottom-up** — Security governance is only effective when driven by the Board of Directors, CEO, and senior management. Security cannot set its own agenda.
- **Board of Directors** — Establishes organizational goals/objectives, sets the tone for governance.
- **CEO** — Directly accountable for corporate governance.
- Security should be a **proactive enabler**, not a reactive function.

### Accountability vs. Responsibility (Table 1-4)

| Accountability | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| "Where the buck stops" | "The doer" |
| Ultimate ownership and liability | In charge of a task or process |
| Only ONE person/group can be accountable | Multiple people can be responsible |
| Sets rules and policies | Develops plans and implements controls |
| **Cannot be delegated** | **Can be delegated** |

Key application: Even if a Cloud Service Provider (CSP) is responsible for protecting data, the **data owner remains accountable**. CSP accountability for data breaches rests with the data owner.

### Scoping and Tailoring

- **Scoping** — Determines which control elements are in scope (applicable) vs. out of scope for the organization.
- **Tailoring** — Takes in-scope controls and refines/enhances them to align with organizational goals and make them cost-effective.

### Organizational Roles (Table 1-5)

| Role | Accountable/Responsible for |
|---|---|
| Owners/Controllers/Senior Mgmt | Ensuring appropriate controls; determining sensitivity/classification; approving access |
| IS Security Professionals | Design, implementation, management of policies, standards, baselines, procedures, guidelines |
| IT Officer | Developing/implementing technology solutions; BCM coordination |
| Information Systems Auditors | Independent assurance that security objectives are appropriate and met |
| Users | Adherence to policies; preserving CIA of assets |

## Exam-Relevant Nuance

- Exam may ask who is "ultimately accountable" for a specific asset. Answer: the **VP/functional leader** for that asset, or if not listed, the next most senior person. The CEO is ultimately accountable for everything if no owner is specified.
- Security is **everyone's responsibility** but accountability always remains with the asset owner/senior management.
- Governance committees should include Legal, HR, IT, and security representatives to ensure alignment.
- The source explicitly states: if Board/CEO don't support security, it becomes "a reactive nuisance versus a proactive enabler."
