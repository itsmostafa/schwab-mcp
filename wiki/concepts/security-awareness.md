# Security Awareness, Education, and Training

Everyone in an organization is responsible for security — but responsibility without knowledge is
insufficient. Awareness, training, and education programs equip people to execute their
security responsibilities.

## Three Levels (Table 1-33)

| Level | Purpose | Characteristics | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Raise cultural sensitivity to security | Organization-wide; less time-intensive | Internal phishing campaigns, lunch-and-learns, security posters |
| **Training** | Provide specific skills for security tasks | More technical; role-focused | Firewall admin learning to write rules; security guard response procedures |
| **Education** | Develop fundamental concepts and decision-making capability | Deepest level; broadest understanding | Security degree programs; CISSP exam preparation |

## Methods and Techniques (§1.12.1)

- Live in-person sessions
- Live online sessions
- Pre-recorded sessions
- **Gamification** — rewards/requirements that make training engaging
- **Security champions** — employees who promote security awareness among peers (e.g., a developer embedded with the security team)
- Regular communications and campaigns

## Prioritization of Topics

- Align training topics with organizational goals, objectives, and the **risk register**.
- The risk register identifies the highest-priority risks, which should drive training priorities.
- Never enough time to cover everything — focus on what matters most to the organization.

## Periodic Content Reviews (§1.12.2)

- Programs must evolve as the threat landscape changes.
- Emerging topics: blockchain/cryptocurrency, AI, cloud risks.
- Review should be triggered by changes in the threat environment, not just calendar schedules.

## Measuring Effectiveness (§1.12.3)

Key metrics:
- Total number completing the program
- Percentage providing feedback
- Number reporting suspicious activities after training
- Pass rates on post-training assessments (e.g., ≥75% threshold)
- Results of simulated phishing exercises (click-through rates before vs. after training)

## Exam-Relevant Nuance

- Exam question: "Best way to mitigate social engineering?" → **Awareness, training, and education** (not technical controls alone).
- Awareness changes **culture**; training provides **skills**; education provides **understanding**.
- Security champions are employees (often developers or business unit staff) who serve as a security bridge to the security team — they are not security professionals themselves.
- "Speak the audience's language" — training for senior management uses business impact language; technical staff training uses technical language.
